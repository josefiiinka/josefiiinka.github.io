When you run into your after-school teacher in the street

A while ago, I took the plunge and found a part-time job at a school's after-school club.

I had my reasons, which I might explore in another article. Today, though, I want to share a brief reflection on something else: How should an after-school teacher actually relate to children?

Our team tends towards a more “old-school” approach. Order, discipline, quiet and clearly defined boundaries come first. The advice given to those of us who are just starting out follows the same lines: set firm boundaries and insist that children respect them.

I am not a great advocate of every form of respectful parenting, but I liked an idea shared by the teacher Štěpánka Cimlová: a teacher should be neither an authority figure nor a friend. Neither harsh nor indifferent. In today's society, a teacher should above all be a partner.

I try to do something similar with my own children.

I don't want them to be afraid of me, but I don't pretend to be their friend either. I try to make sure we talk and work together. At the same time, there are things that simply need doing, and situations in which I guide them towards independence and responsibility.

And I am beginning to feel the same way at the after-school club.

The children aren't always quiet with me. They don't sit neatly and silently the whole time. But I feel that, overall, we have a good relationship. That we understand one another and that they know where the boundaries are with me.

As I was leaving work yesterday, a little girl was walking towards me, and for a moment I thought she was one of the children from my club.

She wasn't.

But that feeling reminded me of being a child myself and running into my teacher in the town square. I was usually shy about talking to her. Almost as if I were afraid she might suddenly ask whether I had finished my homework.

Perhaps this is quite a good measure.

Not whether my club is always absolutely silent. Not whether the children obey me straight away. Not even whether they all like me.

But what happens when we run into one another somewhere outside school.

I would like us to smile, say hello, exchange a few kind words and then each go on our way.

Without awkwardness. Without fear. But still with respect.
