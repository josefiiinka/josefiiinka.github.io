Motherhood as a profession – Finding my own way

From the series Returning to motherhood and healthy eating · Part 2

Can we see maternity leave as a fully fledged job?

“Get up, brush your teeth, put on your make-up and go to work”

When my aunt Klára first told me about her approach to motherhood, I didn't really understand it. It took me some time to appreciate the depth of the idea behind it. Aunt Klára treated maternity leave like a regular job. She got up, brushed her teeth, got ready, put on her make-up and told herself: “I'm going to work. I'm going to be with the children.”

“For her, giving her children care and attention was a profession like any other. I spent a long time trying to adopt this outlook myself, but at first I couldn't make it work. Her approach suited her personality, while I needed to find a way that suited mine.”

We live in an age of courses, but lack a system of our own

We live in a curious age. There are courses, training programmes and guides for almost everything. We learn, study and share experiences. We grew up in an environment that loves systems and order. Yet once we stay at home with children, we often lose that structure and begin to feel overwhelmed or burnt out.

Creating my own structure as a mum

It was coming home from my job at school that opened my eyes. I realised that the answer wasn't to copy other people's instructions or escape into a conventional job. The key was to create my own workable approach to being a mum. One that respects my needs, brings structure to our days and gives motherhood the recognition and dignity it deserves.
