A school for our son? We found one in Albania.

Monday, 26 October 2026

We have finally found a school that caught our attention. In Albania. But our excitement is now mixed with worry. We are watching footage of protests in Tirana: petrol bombs flying, bins burning… and we have booked flights to that very city.

Finally, that feeling: this is it

It is a British school in Tirana. A private school, but still affordable. Certainly compared with the private schools we considered in the Czech Republic. Several teachers we know have recommended this type of school as a potentially good choice for our son.

We watched the promotional videos and finally had that feeling: “This is it.” This was the kind of school we had been looking for.

We excitedly bought our flights and paid for accommodation… and now we are watching live footage from Tirana on YouTube, afraid that our plans might fall through.

Why are we looking for a school outside the Czech Republic?

For several years, we have watched developments in Czech education with sadness. And we still haven't found a school that feels right for our son.

We often miss a more modern approach in mainstream state schools. We see overworked teachers, sometimes burnt out, and an environment where change is hard to bring about. At the mainstream school where I worked, children sat at their desks listening to the teacher's explanation. There was no room left for them to take an active part.

We like alternative schools' vision of treating children with respect. But from what we have seen ourselves, that vision doesn't always translate into practice. Sometimes it feels to us as though respect gives way to chaos, and children set the rules more than we think is healthy.

At one such school, we saw children walking on a cold floor wearing only socks. Two small groups, with a teacher and an assistant, often shared a classroom. The small number of children sounded promising, but the groups disturbed one another and the children struggled to concentrate. These everyday details matter to us just as much as the way a school describes its approach.

The private schools we considered in the Czech Republic are beyond our budget. And even there, we aren't certain that the price reflects the quality we are looking for.

What about home education?

We have thought about that too, but for now we see it as a last resort. For us, it would mean putting our own work and development in the fields we care about on hold for several years. And even after such a major change, we wouldn't be sure it was the right path for our son.

Now we just hope we can go

We still hope the situation in Tirana will calm down and we will be able to travel and visit the school that has interested us so much. So far, we know it only through recommendations and promotional videos. Only by visiting will we discover what an ordinary day there is like, and whether that feeling of “this is it” survives meeting the school in person.

We aren't looking for a perfect school. We want somewhere our son will feel comfortable, be eager to discover new things and receive both the space and the guidance he needs. If we find that place in Albania, it will be a surprise to us too. But we would be happy to be surprised.

With our children, we are gradually entering another important stage of life. And that is precisely why we feel it is worth searching and choosing carefully, even when the journey isn't easy.
